Overview: Winter Wings Over Phobjikha

Each autumn, black-necked cranes leave the Tibetan plateau and glide down into Phobjikha (Gangtey) valley, one of the few wintering grounds for this rare, high-altitude crane species. The valley's wide glacial marshland, protected from development, becomes their feeding ground for the winter months, and the birds' arrival is marked locally with the Black-Necked Crane Festival — a celebration of community dances and crane-themed performances staged by Phobjikha's farming villages.

This departure is timed to catch that arrival window, with dedicated time at the RSPN (Royal Society for Protection of Nature) information centre, where telescopes look out over the marshland, and at the festival itself. Around the crane-watching days, the itinerary takes in the classic western circuit — Punakha's dzong and valley, Thimphu's capital sights, and Paro, including the option to hike to Tiger's Nest Monastery.

It is a single fixed departure built for travellers who want Bhutan's culture paired with one of the Himalaya's most striking winter wildlife spectacles.

Day By Day Itinerary

Arrive at Paro International Airport and transfer to Thimphu, with an easy sightseeing stop en route.

Cross Dochula Pass and its 108 chortens en route to Punakha, visiting Punakha Dzong on arrival.

Drive to Phobjikha (Gangtey) valley, with an afternoon walk on the valley's nature trail looking out for early crane arrivals.

Two days in Phobjikha for crane watching at the RSPN information centre, a visit to Gangtey Monastery, and the Black-Necked Crane Festival.

Return drive to Thimphu, with an afternoon of capital sightseeing including the memorial chorten and Buddha Dordenma statue.

Drive to Paro, with an afternoon walk through Paro town or an optional hike partway up to Tiger's Nest viewpoint.

A relaxed morning before your private transfer to Paro International Airport for your onward flight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Black-necked cranes migrate from the Tibetan plateau into Phobjikha (Gangtey) valley each autumn, with birds typically beginning to arrive around late October and into November. This departure is timed to that arrival window.

The Black-Necked Crane Festival is an annual community event held in Phobjikha valley celebrating the cranes' return, with local dances, crane-themed performances and stalls run by the valley's farming community.

Sightings can never be fully guaranteed as they depend on migration timing and weather, but by early-to-mid November the cranes are typically well established in Phobjikha valley, and the RSPN information centre gives excellent telescope viewing over the marshland where they feed.

The itinerary is timed to the cranes' arrival in Phobjikha valley and to the valley's annual crane festival, both of which fall within a fairly narrow autumn window each year.
