Overview: Bhutan's Most Iconic Festival

Paro Tshechu is the festival most visitors picture when they imagine Bhutan — five days of masked cham dances performed by monks in the courtyard of Rinpung Dzong, against the backdrop of the Paro valley's terraced fields and snow-capped ridgelines. Local families turn out in their finest clothes, and the atmosphere builds steadily across the week towards its closing morning.

On the final day, before sunrise, a giant thongdrel — a sacred appliqued tapestry many storeys tall — is unveiled for a few hours only, drawing pilgrims from across the region who believe that viewing it cleanses the sins of the beholder. This departure is timed so you're in place for that unveiling, alongside several days of festival dances, a hike to Tiger's Nest Monastery, and time to explore the Paro valley at a relaxed pace.

Run as a small group of up to 12 travellers rather than a private tour, it's a sociable way to experience Bhutan's best-known festival alongside a handful of fellow guests.

Day By Day Itinerary

Arrive at Paro International Airport, settle into your hotel and take an orientation walk through Paro town before a welcome dinner.

Visit Paro Dzong and the National Museum before the festival begins, with the afternoon free to relax ahead of the tshechu.

Three days attending festival dances at Rinpung Dzong, with time between sessions to explore the surrounding valley and meet fellow festival-goers.

Rise early for the pre-dawn thongdrel unveiling, then spend the rest of the day on the hike to Taktshang Monastery.

A day trip to Thimphu for the capital's dzong, memorial chorten and market, returning to Paro in the evening.

A relaxed morning before your transfer to Paro International Airport for your onward flight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Paro Tshechu is Bhutan's most iconic festival, held over five days in the courtyard of Rinpung Dzong. It features masked cham dances performed by monks and closes at dawn on the final day with the unveiling of a giant thongdrel, a sacred appliqued tapestry, before sunrise.

A thongdrel is a giant embroidered or appliqued religious tapestry, considered so sacred that simply viewing it is believed to cleanse the viewer of sin. It is displayed only once, briefly before sunrise, so that the fabric is not damaged by direct daylight.
