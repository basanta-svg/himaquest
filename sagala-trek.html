Sagala Trek Overview

The Sagala Trek is a shorter, moderate route linking the Paro valley to remote Haa over Sagala (Saga La) Pass. It sees far fewer trekkers than the well-known Paro-based routes, passing instead through dense dwarf bamboo forest and open yak pastures used by herders who spend their summers on these slopes.

The trail climbs gradually from the Paro side, camping below the pass before crossing Sagala itself at 3,800m, where clear days bring views of Mount Jomolhari and neighbouring Jichu Drake. The descent drops into the quiet, lightly-visited Haa valley, one of Bhutan’s least-developed regions and, until relatively recently, closed to foreign visitors entirely.

This is a fully supported camping trek — a dedicated crew of guides, cooks and pack animals travels with you, pitching camp and preparing meals at each stop.

Trek Highlights

  • Crossing Sagala (Saga La) Pass with views of Jomolhari and Jichu Drake
  • Descending through dense dwarf bamboo forest, unusual among Bhutan’s treks
  • Remote yak-herder camps rarely seen by outside visitors
  • Ending in the untouched, lightly-visited Haa valley
  • A shorter, accessible introduction to high-altitude trekking
  • A fully supported camp with dedicated cook and trek crew

Day By Day Itinerary

Settle into your hotel and meet your trek crew for a gear check and route briefing.

Drive to the trailhead and trek through forest into the foothills below Sagala, camping at Damche.

A steady climb through rhododendron and dwarf bamboo forest to camp just below the pass.

Cross the pass with views of Jomolhari and Jichu Drake on a clear day, then descend into the Haa valley to camp.

A short trek to Haa town, followed by a drive back to Paro or Thimphu for your onward transfer.

What To Pack For The Sagala Trek

Nights near the pass can be cold even in season, so bring a sleeping bag rated to at least -10°C, a down jacket, waterproof shell, thermal layers, sturdy broken-in hiking boots, trekking poles, a headlamp and sun protection. Your trek crew carries tents, kitchen equipment and food, so you’ll only need a daypack for the walking days.
