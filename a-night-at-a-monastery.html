A Night At A Monastery

About This Experience

Most visitors see a Bhutanese monastery for an hour or two — a walk through the courtyard, a glimpse of a shrine room, then back on the road. This experience is different: an overnight stay inside a working monastery, arranged with the resident community, where you eat a simple evening meal alongside the monks, join the tail end of evening ritual, and sleep in a modest guest room within the monastery walls themselves.

The following morning begins early, well before sunrise, with the low pulse of horns and the murmur of chanting drawing you toward the main shrine hall for dawn prayers. You're welcome to sit in and observe, or simply take in the sound of the monastery waking up around you — butter lamps being lit, monks moving quietly between buildings, the valley outside still dark. It's a rare, unhurried window into a rhythm of life that has continued largely unchanged for centuries, offered with the full cooperation and welcome of the community hosting you.

Facilities are simple and monastic by nature — a mattress on the floor, a shared washroom, no heating beyond a blanket — so this experience suits travellers who want an authentic taste of monastic life over creature comforts. Available at a small number of monasteries that welcome overnight guests, subject to the community's schedule and any ongoing retreats.
