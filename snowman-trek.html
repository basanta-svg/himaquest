Snowmen Trek Overview

The Snowman Trek is a continuation of the Laya Gasa route deep into Lunana, one of the most isolated inhabited regions on Earth, reachable only on foot or by helicopter. Crossing eleven passes above 4,500m over roughly 25 days, it is consistently ranked among the hardest treks in the world, and only a small number of trekking groups attempt — let alone complete — it each year.

The route follows the classic Jomolhari and Laya trail as far as Laya, then continues north through Rodophu, Narethang and the settlements of Lunana — Woche, Lhedi and Thanza — beneath Gangkhar Puensum (7,570m), widely believed to be the highest unclimbed mountain in the world. The trek’s highest point, Rinchen Zoe La at 5,320m, is crossed roughly three weeks in, before the route descends toward the Duer Hot Springs and, finally, Bumthang.

Because of its length, altitude and remoteness, this is a fully self-supported expedition-style trek, requiring a large crew, extensive pack animal support and careful contingency planning for weather delays, which are common even within the narrow June-to-September window.

What To Pack For The Snowman Trek

This is a multi-week expedition at sustained high altitude, so gear needs to handle sub-zero nights throughout: a sleeping bag rated to at least -20°C, a heavyweight down jacket, waterproof shell, multiple thermal layers, gaiters, well broken-in mountaineering-grade boots, trekking poles and spares of anything critical (headlamp batteries, blister care). Given the trek’s length and remoteness, a personal first-aid kit, any prescribed altitude medication, and a positive, flexible mindset for weather delays are just as important as physical gear.
