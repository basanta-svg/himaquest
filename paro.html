Paro Dzongkhag

Paro

Home to Tiger's Nest Monastery and Bhutan's only international airport — the valley most travellers see first, and never forget.

About Paro

A wide, fertile valley at around 2,200 metres, Paro is where almost every visitor's journey into Bhutan begins — its airport is the kingdom's only international gateway, and one of the most technically demanding in the world, ringed by peaks that only a handful of specially certified pilots are cleared to fly between. Beyond the runway, Paro is a valley of red-rice paddies and traditional farmhouses, watched over by Rinpung Dzong and, further up the valley, by Taktshang Palphug Monastery — the cliffside "Tiger's Nest" that has come to symbolise Bhutan itself.

Why Visit

  • Taktshang (Tiger's Nest) Monastery, Bhutan's most iconic sight
  • Rinpung Dzong, still an active monastic and administrative fortress
  • The National Museum, housed in the round Ta Dzong watchtower
  • Bhutan's only international airport, ringed by 5,000m peaks
  • Red-rice paddies and traditional farmhouses across the valley floor
  • The colourful, crowd-drawing masked dances of the Paro Tshechu

Getting There

Paro is Bhutan's international gateway — nearly every visitor's first stop in the kingdom, served by direct flights from cities including Delhi, Kathmandu, Bangkok, Singapore and Kolkata. From Paro, it's roughly 1.5 hours by road (about 55km) on to Thimphu, or a scenic drive over Chele La Pass to reach Haa.
