Overview: Caterpillar Fungus & Snow Leopard Country

Each June, as the snow retreats from Bhutan's highest alpine meadows, highland communities from Gasa, Laya and Lunana climb into the mountains to harvest yartsa guenbub — literally "summer grass, winter worm" — the rare caterpillar fungus prized across Asia for its use in traditional medicine. Entire villages camp out for weeks at a time above 4,000m, turning the high pastures into a temporary tent city found nowhere else on the trekking calendar.

These same slopes sit inside Jigme Dorji National Park, one of the most important snow leopard landscapes in the eastern Himalaya. This departure was designed around both: trekking through active harvest camps to meet the families who make this seasonal migration, and moving with a wildlife-trained guide through terrain where camera traps regularly confirm snow leopard activity. It is a rare, narrow-season trip built for travellers who want Bhutan's high mountains at their most alive, rather than at their most photographed.

Because it depends entirely on the harvest season and early-summer conditions, this itinerary is run only once, as a single fixed departure, with a small private group.

Day By Day Itinerary

Arrive in Paro and transfer overland towards Gasa, Bhutan's least-populated dzongkhag, with an overnight stop en route and a full trek briefing.

Visit Gasa Dzong and soak in the natural Tshachu hot springs before beginning the trek proper the following morning.

Trek upward through forest and alpine meadow into the harvest zone, camping alongside or near seasonal yartsa guenbub camps and scanning likely snow leopard terrain with your guide.

Two days based at high camp (up to 4,890m) for wildlife observation, harvest camp visits and acclimatisation, with early-morning and dusk watches for snow leopard sign.

A gradual two-day descent back through the meadows and forest to Gasa, with a final soak in the hot springs on arrival.

Return drive to Paro, with a rest afternoon to clean up gear and unwind before departure.

A relaxed morning before your private transfer to Paro International Airport for your onward flight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Yartsa guenbub, or Ophiocordyceps sinensis, is a rare fungus that grows on ghost moth larvae in high-altitude alpine meadows. Highly valued in traditional medicine, its harvest each June draws highland communities to camp in the mountains for several weeks.

Snow leopards are elusive and sightings can never be guaranteed on any trip. This departure travels through confirmed snow leopard habitat with a wildlife-trained guide, giving you the best realistic chance of signs, camera-trap footage or a sighting, without promising the animal itself.

This is a challenging high-altitude trek, reaching close to 4,900m over multiple consecutive trekking days. A good level of fitness and prior high-altitude trekking experience is recommended.

The route is timed to the short caterpillar fungus harvest season, when highland communities are camped in the high pastures, and to early-summer conditions in snow leopard territory. Outside this narrow window the harvest camps disperse and the route becomes far less accessible.
