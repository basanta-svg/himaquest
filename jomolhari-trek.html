Jomolhari Trek Overview

The Jomolhari Trek is Bhutan's most iconic trekking route — a journey along the Paro Chhu river, through blue pine and rhododendron forest, into the high alpine pastures beneath Mount Jomolhari (7,314m). Revered in Bhutanese culture as the "Bride of Kanchenjunga" and home to the protector goddess Jomo, the mountain dominates the skyline from Jomolhari Base Camp, one of the most photographed views in the Himalaya.

Over 8 days, the trail climbs gradually from Shana Zampa to Jangothang (Jomolhari Base Camp), passing yak-herder settlements, blue sheep grazing on scree slopes, and — with good timing — sightings of the elusive snow leopard's territory. A built-in acclimatisation day at base camp allows for a side hike to the turquoise waters of Tshophu Lake and a closer viewpoint of the peak, before the trail retraces its steps back to Shana Zampa and Paro.

This is a fully supported camping trek — a dedicated crew of guides, cooks and pack animals travels with you, pitching camp and preparing meals at each stop, so the only thing you need to focus on is the trail ahead.

Day By Day Itinerary

Land at Paro's dramatic mountain-ringed airport, settle into your hotel and meet your trek crew for a full gear check and route briefing over dinner.

Drive an hour to the trailhead at Shana Zampa, then trek roughly 17km along the Paro Chhu through farmland and blue pine forest to camp at Thangthangka.

The tree line thins as the valley opens up, with your first full views of Mount Jomolhari. Camp at Jangothang, directly beneath the mountain, near the ruins of Jomolhari Dzong.

A rest day at base camp built in for altitude acclimatisation, with an optional day hike up to Tshophu Lake and the Bonte La viewpoint (4,080m) for the trek's closest views of Jomolhari and neighbouring Jichu Drake.

Begin the descent, retracing the trail back through the valley to Thangthangka, with the afternoon light on the peaks behind you.

The final trekking day back to the trailhead at Shana Zampa, followed by a short drive back to Paro for a well-earned hot shower and proper bed.

A buffer day built into the itinerary for weather delays, or — if all has gone to plan — an optional hike to Tiger's Nest Monastery to round off the trip.

A relaxed morning before your private transfer to Paro International Airport for your onward flight.

What To Pack For The Jomolhari Trek

Nights at Jangothang can drop well below freezing even in peak season, so layering matters more than any single piece of gear. Pack a warm sleeping bag rated to at least -10°C, a down jacket, waterproof shell, thermal base layers, sturdy broken-in hiking boots, trekking poles, a headlamp, sun protection and a basic personal first-aid kit. Your trek crew carries tents, kitchen equipment and food, so you'll only need a daypack for the walking day itself.
