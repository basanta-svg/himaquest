Drukpath Trek Overview

The Druk Path Trek is one of Bhutan’s most popular short treks, linking Paro and Thimphu along a high ridge strung with alpine lakes. It packs a genuine high-altitude experience — forest, lakes, yak pasture and panoramic Himalayan views — into just 6 days, making it an ideal choice for trekkers with limited time or a more moderate fitness level.

From the trailhead near Paro’s National Museum, the trail climbs to Jele Dzong before following a ridge past a chain of lakes, including Jimilang Tsho and Simkotra Tsho, both known for their trout. The highest point, near Phume La at 4,200m, offers sweeping views that stretch to Mount Jomolhari on a clear day.

The final descent drops through rhododendron forest past Phajoding Monastery, a working monastic community perched above the Thimphu valley, before reaching the capital itself. This is a fully supported camping trek, with a dedicated crew of guides, cooks and pack animals.

Day By Day Itinerary

Settle into your hotel and meet your trek crew for a gear check and route briefing.

Trek up through blue pine forest from Paro’s Ta Dzong (National Museum) to camp near Jele Dzong.

Follow a scenic ridge with views of Paro valley below and, on clear days, Jomolhari in the distance.

Continue along the ridge, watching for yak herds, to camp beside the trout-filled Jimilang Tsho.

Cross the trek’s highest point at Phume La, with the best mountain views of the trek, before camping beside Simkotra Tsho.

Descend through forest past Phajoding Monastery, arriving in Thimphu by early afternoon.

What To Pack For The Druk Path Trek

Nights near the lakes and Phume La can be cold, so bring a sleeping bag rated to at least -10°C, a down jacket, waterproof shell, thermal layers, sturdy broken-in hiking boots, trekking poles, a headlamp and sun protection. Anglers may wish to bring gear — a local fishing permit can be arranged in advance. Your trek crew carries tents, kitchen equipment and food, so you’ll only need a daypack for the walking days.
