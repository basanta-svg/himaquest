Overview: Highland Festival & The Classic West

Each October, Gasa's high pastures fill with yak caravans and highland families for the Royal Highland Festival — a two-day celebration of Bhutan's remote highlander communities, staged above 4,000m against a backdrop of snow peaks. Yak dressing competitions, archery, traditional highland sport and craft stalls bring together herders from Gasa, Laya, Lunana and beyond, most of whom otherwise rarely gather in one place.

This departure pairs that rare highland gathering with Bhutan's most-loved western circuit. From Gasa the route drops back down through Punakha's fertile valley to its dzong at the confluence of two rivers, over Dochula Pass with its 108 chortens and, weather permitting, Himalayan views, and on to Thimphu and Paro for the country's cultural heart and the hike to Tiger's Nest Monastery.

It is a single itinerary built around two very different sides of Bhutan — the wind-scoured highlands and the greener, more populated valleys below — run once a year to match the festival's fixed dates.

Trip Highlights

  • Two full days at the Royal Highland Festival among Gasa's yak-herding communities
  • Cross Dochula Pass and its 108 memorial chortens
  • Visit Punakha Dzong, Bhutan's most photogenic fortress-monastery
  • Hike to Taktshang (Tiger's Nest) Monastery above the Paro valley
  • Explore Thimphu's markets, dzong and memorial chorten
  • A private guide and vehicle throughout, timed precisely to the festival dates

Day By Day Itinerary

Arrive at Paro International Airport and transfer to Thimphu, with an easy sightseeing stop en route and a welcome briefing over dinner.

A scenic drive north to Gasa, Bhutan's least-populated dzongkhag, arriving in time to settle in ahead of the festival.

Two full days at the festival grounds, watching yak competitions, archery and highland sport, and meeting herder families from across Bhutan's high valleys.

Descend from the highlands to Punakha, with a visit to Gasa Dzong and its hot springs before continuing on.

Visit Punakha Dzong and take a short walk through rice terraces to Chimi Lhakhang, the temple of the "Divine Madman".

Cross Dochula Pass and its 108 chortens en route back to Thimphu, with an afternoon of capital sightseeing.

Continue sightseeing in Thimphu before an afternoon drive to Paro and a walk through Paro town.

A half-day hike to Taktshang Monastery, perched on a cliff face above the Paro valley, followed by a relaxed afternoon.

A relaxed morning before your private transfer to Paro International Airport for your onward flight.

Frequently Asked Questions

The Royal Highland Festival is an annual gathering held above 4,000m in Gasa, bringing together yak-herding communities from across Bhutan's highlands for two days of yak competitions, archery, traditional highland sport and craft displays. It celebrates the culture and livelihoods of Bhutan's remote highlander population.

The festival grounds sit above 4,000m and the drive there involves some walking at altitude, but this is a touring itinerary rather than a trek — you will be based in hotels throughout and travel mostly by vehicle. A reasonable level of fitness is helpful, but no trekking experience is required.

After the festival, the itinerary follows Bhutan's classic western circuit — Punakha's dzong and valley, the 108 chortens at Dochula Pass, and Thimphu and Paro's cultural sites, including a hike to Tiger's Nest Monastery.

The Royal Highland Festival is held on specific dates set by the organisers each year, so this itinerary is built around that fixed window. Outside these dates the festival itself is not taking place.
