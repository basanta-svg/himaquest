Dagala Trek Overview

The Dagala Thousand Lakes Trek is the most accessible high-altitude trek from Thimphu, climbing out of the capital into the Dagala range’s rolling alpine country, dotted with dozens of glacial lakes that give the trek its name. It is a quieter alternative to the Paro-based routes, with far fewer trekkers on the trail and camps pitched beside still, trout-filled waters.

Over 6 days, the route climbs through blue pine and rhododendron forest before opening onto high pasture used by yak herders each summer. Two nights are spent near the Labatama lake basin, from where clear days reveal views stretching from Chomolhari to Kanchenjunga, and occasionally Everest, far to the west.

This is a fully supported camping trek — a dedicated crew of guides, cooks and pack animals travels with you, pitching camp and preparing meals at each stop, leaving you free to focus on the trail and the views.

Day By Day Itinerary

Settle into your hotel in the capital and meet your trek crew for a gear check and route briefing over dinner.

Drive roughly an hour to the trailhead at Geynizampa, then trek through blue pine and rhododendron forest to the first camp at Gur.

Climb steadily past Jichu Draka, entering high alpine country as the trail nears the Labatama lake basin, tonight’s camp.

A day to explore the surrounding lakes — including Dala Tsho and Utsho — with optional trout fishing and, on clear days, views of Chomolhari and Kanchenjunga from the surrounding ridgelines (4,720m).

Cross a high ridge with views over both the Thimphu and Paro valleys before descending to camp near Talakha Monastery.

A final descent through forest back to the trailhead, followed by a drive back into Thimphu where the trek concludes.

What To Pack For The Dagala Thousand Lakes Trek

Nights beside the lakes can drop well below freezing even in peak season, so pack a warm sleeping bag rated to at least -10°C, a down jacket, waterproof shell, thermal base layers, sturdy broken-in hiking boots, trekking poles, a headlamp, sun protection and a basic personal first-aid kit. Anglers should bring their own gear — a local fishing permit can be arranged in advance. Your trek crew carries tents, kitchen equipment and food, so you’ll only need a daypack for each walking day.
