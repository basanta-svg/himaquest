When To Go

Best Time To Visit

Mar – May

Spring

Rhododendrons and magnolias bloom across the valleys, mountain views are clear, and the festival calendar begins with the Paro Tshechu.

Jun – Aug

Summer

The monsoon brings lush, deep-green valleys and fewer crowds — a quieter, more affordable season for lower-altitude cultural touring.

Sep – Nov

Autumn

Bhutan's most popular season: crystal-clear skies, the best trekking conditions, and major tshechus in Thimphu and across the country.

Dec – Feb

Winter

Crisp, cold days under blue skies, snow on the high passes, and the black-necked cranes arriving in Phobjikha valley.

Good To Know

The Sustainable Development Fee

Every visitor to Bhutan contributes a Sustainable Development Fee (SDF), which directly funds free healthcare and education for Bhutanese citizens, environmental conservation, and infrastructure across the kingdom's 20 dzongkhags.

USD 100 Per adult, per night
USD 50 Children aged 6–11, per night
Free Children under 6
Pack Smart

What To Pack

  • Layered clothing for changing altitudes through the day
  • A warm jacket — evenings stay cool even in summer
  • Comfortable, broken-in walking shoes
  • Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat for high-altitude sun
  • A modest outfit for temple and dzong visits
  • A universal power adapter
Arrival & Getting Around

Getting There

Paro International Airport is Bhutan's only international gateway, served by Drukair and Bhutan Airlines from Delhi, Kolkata, Bangkok, Singapore, Kathmandu and Dhaka — one of the most spectacular landings in commercial aviation.

All travel within Bhutan is by private vehicle with a licensed driver and guide, arranged as part of your itinerary. For longer journeys, domestic flights and helicopter transfers connect Paro, Bumthang and Gelephu.
