Bumthang Dzongkhag

Bumthang

The spiritual heartland of Bhutan — four sacred valleys of ancient temples, apple orchards and the highest concentration of important monasteries in the kingdom.

About Bumthang

Made up of four valleys — Chumey, Choekhor, Tang and Ura — Bumthang is where Bhutanese Buddhism runs deepest. It is home to some of the country's oldest temples, including Jambay Lhakhang and Kurjey Lhakhang, both linked to the 8th-century arrival of Guru Rinpoche. At an average elevation of around 2,600 to 3,000 metres, the valleys stay cool and pastoral, with red-and-white farmhouses scattered between buckwheat fields, apple orchards and pine forest.

Why Visit

  • Bhutan's highest concentration of sacred temples and monasteries
  • Four distinct valleys, each with its own character and pace
  • Local honey, cheese and apple brandy from small family farms
  • The Wangdicholing Palace and Jakar Dzong overlooking the valley
  • Home of the Ura Yakchoe and Jambay Lhakhang Drup festivals
  • Quiet farmhouse stays and gentle valley walks

Getting There

Bumthang is reached by road from Thimphu, a scenic drive of roughly seven to eight hours over the Pele La and Yotong La passes, or via a short domestic flight from Paro to Bumthang Domey Airport. Most itineraries pair Bumthang with Trongsa and Wangdue Phodrang as part of a central Bhutan loop.
