Laya Gasa Trek Overview

The Laya Gasa Trek is one of Bhutan’s classic long treks, following the Jomolhari route north through Jigme Dorji National Park before continuing deep into the high country toward Laya — home to the Layap people, distinguished by their pointed bamboo hats and semi-nomadic yak-herding life. It is one of the few treks in the world that still passes through genuinely lived-in, largely self-sufficient mountain communities.

Beyond Jomolhari Base Camp, the trail climbs over a series of high passes — Nyile La, Gombu La, Jare La and Sinche La, the trek’s highest point at 5,005m — linking remote settlements at Lingshi, Chebisa, Shomuthang and Robluthang. Blue sheep are a common sight on the scree slopes, and the area is also home to snow leopard, though sightings are rare.

After Laya, the route descends gradually to Gasa, where a soak in the natural hot springs (tshachu) beneath Gasa Dzong is a fitting reward after nearly two weeks on the trail. This is a fully supported camping trek throughout, with a dedicated crew of guides, cooks and pack animals.

Trek Highlights

  • The remote village of Laya, home to the distinctive Layap people and their conical bamboo hats
  • Crossing Sinche La (5,005m), the trek’s highest point
  • Lingshi Dzong, perched dramatically above the Lingshi valley
  • Soaking in Gasa’s natural hot springs (tshachu) at trail’s end
  • Chances of spotting blue sheep, and in the right season, signs of snow leopard
  • Remote, sparsely visited high-altitude villages inside Jigme Dorji National Park

Day By Day Itinerary

Land at Paro and meet your trek crew for a full gear check and route briefing.

Drive to the trailhead at Shana Zampa, then trek along the Paro Chhu through forest to camp at Thangthangka.

The valley opens up with first views of Mount Jomolhari. Camp at Jangothang, beneath the mountain.

A rest day at base camp with an optional side hike to Tshophu Lake and the Bonte La viewpoint.

Cross the trek’s first big pass, descending into the remote Lingshi valley beneath its dramatically-sited dzong.

A shorter, gentler day past yak pastures and a small waterfall to the village of Chebisa.

Climb over Gombu La with a good chance of spotting blue sheep, descending to camp at Shomuthang.

A demanding day crossing Jare La, with views back toward Jomolhari on clear mornings.

The trek’s highest pass and toughest day, rewarded by views of Gangchhenta (Great Tiger Mountain) on the descent.

A gentler descent through forest to the remote Layap village of Laya.

A full day to rest and explore Laya’s villages, meeting the Layap community and, if timed well, their traditional dress and customs.

Descend along the Mo Chhu valley through rhododendron and bamboo forest.

The final trekking day, arriving at Gasa in time to visit Gasa Dzong and soak in the natural hot springs.

Drive out from Gasa via Punakha to Paro or Thimphu for your onward transfer or flight.

What To Pack For The Laya Gasa Trek

This is a long, high-altitude trek, so a warm sleeping bag rated to at least -15°C, a good down jacket, waterproof shell, thermal layers, gaiters, sturdy broken-in boots and trekking poles are essential. Bring blister care, sun protection, a headlamp and any personal medication, including altitude sickness tablets if prescribed. Your trek crew carries tents, kitchen equipment and food, so a daypack is all you’ll need on the trail.
