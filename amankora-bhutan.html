The Amankora Bhutan Journey Overview

Amankora operates five lodges across Bhutan's central and western valleys, each built by architect Kerry Hill in a shared language of rammed earth, stone and pine that echoes the region's dzongs and farmhouses while staying entirely its own. The name itself sets the tone: "aman" means peace, and "kora" is the Buddhist term for a pilgrimage circuit — so a stay across all five lodges traces its own kora through the kingdom, from Paro's pine forests to Bumthang's sacred valleys.

Across the five lodges — Paro (24 suites), Thimphu (16 suites), Punakha (8 suites, set in a 300-year-old farmhouse), Gangtey (8 suites, overlooking Gangtey Goemba) and Bumthang (16 suites) — the design stays consistent while the setting changes completely, from forest to farmland to glacial valley. Every lodge has its own spa drawing on traditional Bhutanese healing and hot stone bath rituals, alongside Bhutanese and international dining built around each valley's produce.

This is a fully private, fully customisable journey — the lodge order, length of stay and daily activities can all be shaped around how you want to travel, whether that means lingering longer at the spa or spending more time exploring the valleys between lodges.

Day By Day Itinerary

Land at Paro's mountain-ringed airport and transfer through pine forest to Amankora Paro, with the afternoon free to settle in and explore the lodge's two-storey spa.

A guided hike up to Taktshang, Bhutan's most famous monastery clinging to a cliff face, then an afternoon at leisure back at the lodge.

Drive to Thimphu, checking into the forest-set lodge overlooking the capital, with time to visit Buddha Dordenma and Tashichho Dzong.

A relaxed day between the capital's markets and landmarks and Amankora Thimphu's spa, with a traditional hot stone bath on offer in the evening.

Cross Dochula Pass and its 108 memorial chortens before descending into the warm Punakha valley to a lodge built within a 300-year-old farmhouse above the rice terraces.

Visit Punakha Dzong at the confluence of two rivers, then walk across one of Bhutan's longest suspension bridges before an afternoon at leisure by the farmhouse lodge.

Climb into the Black Mountains to the glacial cirque of Gangtey (Phobjikha), checking into the lodge overlooking 16th-century Gangtey Goemba monastery.

A gentle walk through the farmland and hiking trails surrounding the valley, home to the rare black-necked cranes that winter here between November and February.

Continue east into Bumthang, Bhutan's spiritual heartland, checking into the lodge beside the Royal Wangdichholing Palace.

Visit Jakar Dzong and Jambay Lhakhang, one of Bhutan's oldest temples, before flying out from Bumthang's domestic airport or driving back toward Paro for your onward journey.

What Makes This Journey Different

Amankora is designed around the idea of a kora — a circuit walked slowly, valley by valley, rather than a checklist of hotel stays. The five lodges share one architectural language but sit in entirely different settings, from Paro's pine forest to Punakha's farmhouse terraces to Gangtey's open glacial valley, so the journey itself becomes part of the experience rather than the transfers between it. Your guide paces each day around the weather, your energy and how much time you'd rather spend at the spa versus out in the valley.
