Festivals Of Bhutan

A Kingdom That Celebrates Its Faith

Experience the true charm of a Bhutanese festival on a custom-built journey, where you can witness vivid traditional attire and mesmerising masked dances while immersing yourself in an authentic local celebration. Few things reveal the soul of the kingdom quite like standing in a dzong courtyard as an entire community gathers for days of ritual, colour and devotion.

What Is A Tshechu?

Bhutanese festivals are called Tshechus — literally, "the tenth day" of the Bhutanese lunar calendar, the auspicious date associated with Guru Rinpoche, the saint who brought Buddhism to Bhutan. On this day, monks and laypeople perform sacred cham, masked dances that re-enact stories of good triumphing over evil, while atsaras — clowns in red masks — move through the crowd offering comic relief and keeping order.

Tshechus are as much a social occasion as a religious one. Villagers dress in their finest kira and gho, don family heirloom jewellery, and gather to socialise, share food and seek blessings and merit for the year ahead. Because the calendar follows lunar reckoning, exact dates shift from year to year — which is why we've included the confirmed tentative dates for 2026 and 2027 below.

Not To Miss

Festivals Worth Building A Trip Around

Paro • March–April

Paro Tshechu

Bhutan's most iconic festival, held in the courtyard of Rinpung Dzong. Five days of masked dances close with the pre-dawn unfurling of a giant sacred thongdrel, believed to cleanse the sins of all who see it.

Thimphu • September–October

Thimphu Tshechu

The capital's largest gathering, drawing thousands of pilgrims to Tashichho Dzong for three days of cham performed by monks of the Central Monastic Body.

Gasa / Laya • October

Royal Highland Festival

Held at over 4,000 metres among Bhutan's yak-herding highlanders, this festival celebrates nomadic culture with yak competitions, archery and traditional highland sport.

Trongsa • December–January

Trongsa Tshechu

A winter festival at the ancestral seat of the Wangchuck dynasty, staged in the courtyard of Bhutan's largest dzong against a backdrop of snow-dusted hills.

Punakha • February

Punakha Drubchen & Tshechu

A dramatic re-enactment of a 17th-century battle against Tibetan invaders, staged by local militia in period armour, followed immediately by the more traditional Punakha Tshechu.

Bumthang • October–November

Jambay Lhakhang Drup

One of Bhutan's most sacred festivals, held at a temple said to date to the 7th century, famous for its midnight fire ritual and the Mewang fire-blessing ceremony.

Gasa • October

Gasa Tshechu

A small, little-visited festival at a remote fortress-dzong near the hot springs, offering an intimate alternative to the larger tshechus further south.

Punakha (Talo village) • April–May

Talo Tshechu

A village-scale celebration on a hillside above Punakha, prized by travellers for its unhurried, community feel far from the festival crowds.
